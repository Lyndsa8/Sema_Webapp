1. System Context & Foundational Assumptions

SEMA (Sema Safaricom) is an omni-channel customer intelligence and rapid incident dispatch platform architected to eliminate telecommunication response latency across Kenya. Operating in high-density metropolitan and rural corridors, SEMA bridges citizen problem statements across voice recordings, photographic evidence, and CRM feeds into verified NOC directives.

ASSUMPTION 01 Kenyan Multilingual Continuum
Subscribers code-switch fluidly between English, Kiswahili, and Sheng. The system must never reject colloquial dialect (e.g., "manze router inawaka red", "chapaa imekwama kwa paybill") and must generate parallel normalized English translations for field engineers without losing citizen intent.
ASSUMPTION 02 Client-Side Multimodal Edge
Mobile subscribers capture high-resolution camera images (8–12 MB) of optical network terminals (ONT), error dialogues, or app stalls. Browser edge processing compresses files to ~1 MB via HTML5 Canvas rasterization before single-request transit, ensuring zero mobile bandwidth waste.
ASSUMPTION 03 Zero-Trust Privacy & Fraud Safety
Customer reports and uploaded screenshots must never retain or request M-PESA PINs, full National ID numbers, or OTP credentials. All SIM swap or fraud indicators immediately trigger emergency freezes routed exclusively to the Cyber-Fraud Unit.
ASSUMPTION 04 Cascading Failure Correlation
Fiber backhaul outages in a ward inevitably trigger secondary spikes in M-PESA merchant settlement timeouts and mobile cell overload. Spatial-temporal clustering correlates divergent categories as "Possibly related (same area and time)".

Persistence & State In-Memory Registry + LocalStorage Stateless container resilience with browser local caching. Provides immediate response latency without external database handshake dependencies.
4. Spatial-Temporal Clustering & "Possible Cause" Heuristics

Rather than presenting naive single-ticket lists, SEMA groups incoming incidents by geographic county/ward using a 60-minute sliding window. Any area accumulating ≥3 tickets automatically triggers cluster correlation:

ALGORITHM Category Breakdown & Ratios
Each cluster computes a strict category breakdown (e.g., Fibre 8 · M-PESA 5). The highest-count category is established as the primary driver, while secondary categories are highlighted with the badge:
Possibly related (same area and time)
Accompanied by a 1-line AI contextual hypothesis explaining how fixed backhaul failures knocked out local merchant payment callbacks.
LABELING STANDARD Possible Cause (AI Hypothesis)
In full adherence to engineering precision, the diagnostic heading is titled "Possible cause (AI hypothesis)". This clarifies to NOC managers that the correlation is an intelligent predictive hypothesis based on telecom topology, avoiding premature assumption of physical severed lines until confirmed by field OTDR instruments.

6. Security, Anti-Fraud & Governance Safeguards

1. Customer Protection Note: The customer portal explicitly mandates: "Do not include PINs, ID numbers or full M-PESA messages."
2. AI Automated Scrubbing: Gemini prompt configurations filter out inadvertent biometric, national identity, or authorization tokens before draft generation.
3. Resilient Non-Blocking UX: If vision OCR is inconclusive or the image is unrelated, SEMA prompts the citizen to confirm typed text. Ticket submission is never blocked by an upstream AI error.
